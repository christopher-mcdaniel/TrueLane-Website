TrueLane Desk

Dispatch and fleet software
The Desk page
The problem
Dispatch tools show you the load, not the money, and the numbers live in five places that never match.
What I built
One system for quoting, dispatch, billing, driver pay, a live fleet map and an audit log. Every offer is priced against that truck’s own cost. A rate confirmation is read into a draft load by an AI service, and a person checks every field before it is saved. Spend limits cap what the AI reading can cost.
Built with
Next.js, TypeScript, Prisma and Postgres with row-level security, with automated tests.
How I checked it
Run against invented data at 1,500 tractors, 5,000 trailers and 50,000 loads, with most pages opening in about a second.

TrueLane driver app

iOS, in TestFlight beta
The app page
The problem
Owner-operators say yes to loads that look good and lose money, because a rate means nothing without the miles, the costs and the empty return.
What I built
An offline-first app that sets every offer against the driver’s own cost per mile and states the basis it used, reads rate confirmations into loads, and carries the work through trip tracking, invoicing, and tax and expense records.
Built with
Expo and React Native, TypeScript, Postgres with row-level security, thousands of automated tests.
Where it stands
In TestFlight beta, not in the App Store yet.

Booking-plan platform

Internal tool, Ashley Furniture Distribution Services
The problem
Backhaul booking ran across five disconnected tools, with duplicate entry and recurring data errors.
What I built
I mapped the workflow with DMAIC, baselined the admin time and the errors, then designed and built a replacement: a validated schema, document ingestion with human review, per-load documentation gates, cancellation reason codes and full audit logging, backed by an ~11,500-word specification.
How I checked it
More than 500 automated regression tests. In the same role I rebuilt the regional KPI workbook, found a silent formula defect that left two lanes out of the regional totals, and added automated reconciliation checks.
Result
Duplicate manual entry across systems was eliminated. The region’s revenue per mile went from $0.04 to $0.72 against a $0.65 goal. This is an employer’s internal tool, so there is no public demo.
